# Kernel defects found 2026-11-16 (G5 rebuild)

1. **Mass moments were wrong on Bezier-patch faces and said certified.** `mass` on a skinned turbine blade printed the octree volume 94.5715 (OCCT 94.5715) and a `moments volume=[31.6358, ...] status=certified` line with a wrong centroid: `src/k3/div.bend` had no patch path, so a patch face fell through to the torus formula, and its error bound only compared two quadratures of that wrong integrand. Fixed: Green's theorem in (u, v) with Gauss 8 inner integrals and a 96/48-step boundary walk with a Richardson bound; an unsupported surface now refuses the moments line. Reproducer `tests/std/moments_patch`.
2. **A union of two coaxial cylinders sharing an end plane is refused (K1 reason 92).** A 0.6 bore and a 1.05 socket in a fitting, both ending at z = 6.3: `boundaries-cross-or-overlap: face-penetration`. A stepped bore is an everyday tool body. Reproducer `tests/briefs/repro/union_coaxial_shared_cap` (cylinder r 0.6 x 15.6 from z -9.3 united with r 1.05 x 3.3 from z 3). The probe `tests/k3/zk1r.bend` found no coplanar pair and both caps classified "on" (code 3): the caps sit at -9.3 + 15.6 = 6.299999999999999 and 3 + 3.3 = 6.3, one ulp apart, and `FB.coplanar` demanded bit-exact equality while the classifier's on-band is 2^-40 relative. Fixed in r1o: `FB.coplanar` accepts parallel planes whose offset is inside that same band. The union commits with [25.3408717420, 25.3408717420], the exact π(0.36·15.6 + 1.1025·3.3 − 0.36·3.3); OCCT's fuse gives 25.3408715, and its own volume integration is 1.3e-7 low even on the plain bore cylinder. The G5 fitting still cuts the stepped bore as two sequential differences; that workaround can now go.
3. **The freeform mesher is dense, slow and leaves zero-area triangles.** One turbine blade (19 bicubic faces) meshes to 47,564 triangles against an estimate of 2,478 in 30 s, with 80 zero-area triangles. Near one corner 12 vertices converge geometrically (gaps 3.4e-7 down to 2e-13 mm): refinement bisects the same edge forever because the patch normal unit(su x sv) is undefined where a derivative vanishes on the boundary, so the angle test never passes. Fix in `src/c06/tess.bend`: the limit normal just inside the patch where su x sv degenerates, and no angle-driven split of an edge shorter than the chord tolerance (`tests/mesh/freeform.py`). A first guess (the flip guard's orientation sign on a degenerate side) was a real defect too but changed nothing here.
5. **Joints fail on the front cover and the fittings' modelled threads.** (The front cover's `face-face-uncertain 28 30` is gone on r1s: the front cover commits in 1534 s with the screwed joints on.) With the screwed joints the front cover's last cut is rejected by validation (`face-face-uncertain 28 30`), and `xthread` on the M5 fittings refuses `thread-model-breaks-out` because the hex shoulder at the thread's end plane is counted as reaching the thread shell. Open; the readiness run carries the threaded ports and fittings as semantic M5 callouts and leaves the screwed joints for after these fixes.
4. **Bladed parts take 10 to 60 minutes.** NGV ring 620 s, turbine 740 s, liner 800 s, impeller over 46 min on `p9q`. In the NGV, 19 vanes unite in 59 s and the hub-shroud union with them takes about 560 s; the probe puts 258 s in result validation (`vcore`), 67 s in emit and 30 s in chains. Open.

## Found and fixed 2026-11-17

6. **`mass` spent all its time in the depth-4 octree on patch bodies.** On the G5 impeller hub with one blade it ran for more than 30 min, and the octree certified no cell inside. With `mass 19 0` the same part took 11 s: certified volume and divergence moments. Fixed in r1j. A body with Bezier-patch faces whose moments certify defaults to octree depth 0; an explicit DEPTH still runs the octree.
7. **Coincident mesh vertices about 1e-13 apart made zero-area fillers.** The r1g weld swept a lexicographic sort and missed them. r1h welds within 1e-6 using a generic projection key and a window of recent representatives. The blade went from 133 zero-area triangles to 0.
8. **Edges were sampled by chord only.** Since the face refiner never splits a boundary edge, it bisected toward an under-sampled edge until points landed on the boundary curve. The result was skirt triangles over the planar caps, with edges used up to 32 times. r1i bounds a Bezier edge's turning by its control polygon. The freeform blade is now conforming: 24,486 triangles, every edge used twice, volume within 0.12%. The G5 NGV is conforming too (it had 5,298 zero-area triangles). The impeller has no zero-area triangle left (was 116,024), with 258 edges still over-used; the turbine has none either (was 179,072), with 57. r1k additionally samples every edge of a patch face by that face's normal turning along it: a twisted patch turns its normal along a straight edge.
9. **The clearance bound treated a cone face as its whole meridian rectangle.** A blade 0.27 mm from a conical shroud whose face spans 0.5 mm radially could never get a positive lower bound: `clearance [0, 0.2727]`, and `interf` said `relation uncertain`. r1k bounds the cone by its meridian line ρ = k|h − h₀| as an exact interval, alongside the existing cylinder line and torus/sphere circle bounds.
10. **Whole-part rotor/stator clearance takes hours.** Turbine 27 against NGV 26 ran for more than 4 h. Measured instead: one blade against the stator's surface of revolution, which gives the same certified gap for every blade at every angle. G5 turbine tip: [0.30179, 0.30179] in 0.3 s.
11. **Booleans refused on G5 geometry the design needs.** Open K1 defects. The G5 design works around them, and each workaround is listed here so it can be removed when the kernel is fixed:
    - a revolve's seam line crossing a patch face, K1 reason 72 (the straightener ring is rotated half a pitch off the plate seam);
    - a cylinder tangent to a bore, edge-crossing-uncertain (tunnel boss radius 3.5 to 3.4, which was exactly tangent to the 15.5 bore);
    - one union of a disjoint-blade tree with bosses that overlap the vanes, reason 72 (the diffuser is united in stages: vanes, bosses, tunnel bosses, straighteners).
12. **The gprof profiling build has no profiler, but a sampler does exist.** The gprof build links without the `-pg` startup, so no gmon.out is written, and WSL has no `perf`. None of that is needed: `tools/speed/sample.py` (since 900f43d) samples a kernel run through ptrace, and with `SAMPLE_KSTK` set to the build's generated C it walks the Bend runtime stack for callers. A build from a /var/tmp copy of the compiler that names its specialized `spin_*` segments after their defs makes the flat profile readable. Measured on the G6 hub with one blade, 922 s: 12.5% average thread use, 43% of busy samples in reference counting (`term_drop`, `span_fade`, `rfc_wrap`), and nearly all of the rest in the C07 interval solver (`IVX_VDOT`, `LIN_*` Gauss-Jordan, `ENG_KRAW_Y`) on list-based vectors.
13. **A skinned vane through a revolved wall was refused (K1 reason 72).** The G5 diffuser plate (a revolve) united with one straightener vane, a ruled skin whose root is embedded 1 mm inside the 45.5-47 wall, failed with `edge-face-penetration ... [k1 reason 72]`. p7d refused it the same way, so it was not a regression. The probe `tests/k3/zfr.bend` printed every trace of the plate-cylinder × vane-patch pair. Every traced point certified (9 needed, 0 failed). The trace itself failed: the backward march reached the patch's u = 0 edge (u = −7e-6), and its next fixed step of 0.126 mm landed on the extrapolated patch, where Newton cannot converge. The tracer abandoned the curve there instead of retrying. Fixed in r1n: `fr_go` in `src/k1/ss.bend` halves the step on a failed step, down to 1/64 of the nominal step, and grows it back after each accepted point. Traces that never fail a step are unchanged point for point. The union now commits in 4 s with volume [30424.53175, 30424.53338]. OCCT's own fuse of the two operands gives 30424.53243, and OCCT reads the exported union back as valid. The reproducer moved to `tests/briefs/repro/union_vane_through_wall`.

## Found and fixed 2026-11-18 (G6 impeller and stator)

14. **A K1 refusal costs half an hour before it is reported.** The G6 hub with one blade was refused with K1 reason 81 in about 2 s (`tests/k3/zstage.bend`, line-buffered with `stdbuf -oL`). `run_intersect_cls` then validated the two operands appended into one B-rep with the C07 interval machinery, which ran for more than 30 minutes, only to call K1 again (non-strict, same answer). Open as a speed item: the second K1 attempt can only change the outcome when K1 found nothing at all (reason 98).
15. **The G6 blade's trailing face was tangent to the hub rim (a design flaw, K1 reason 81).** `tests/k3/zn81.bend` lists every near-tangent edge-face pair: the hub's circle at r = 100, z = 66 touched the blade's flat trailing face x = 100 at (100, 0, 66), because the blade trailing edge sat exactly on the hub's outer radius. The hub contour now runs its last segment to a rim 0.5 mm beyond R2 (`HUB_RIM` in `tools/g6/humphrey.py`); R2, the aerodynamic tip radius, is unchanged.
16. **Validation was uncertain on two coaxial cones meeting at under a degree.** With the tangency gone, the G6 hub and blade union was rejected with `face-face-uncertain` between two unchanged back-face cones of the hub (slopes 0.296 and 0.281 at r = 50), where the generic interval tracer is ill-conditioned. Fixed in r1p: the face-face check now treats coaxial cylinder/cone pairs exactly. Their meridian profiles ρ = α + βt cross at t = (α₂ − α₁)/(β₁ − β₂), and each circle there is checked with the same membership test the plane-revolution path uses. Near-parallel profiles fall back to the generic path. Reproducer `tests/briefs/repro/union_hub_blade_rim`. It commits in 3 s against OCCT's fuse 445434.0324, where it was refused after more than 30 minutes.
17. **Plane-cone traces missed cuts away from a 30° grid line (K1 reason 93).** A splitter 15° off the hub seam was refused because one hub face's cuts did not close. Its flat trailing face crosses the last hub cone within ±0.8° of θ = 15°, and the tracer seeded a 12-column θ grid over the full circle, so it never seeded that curve. The blade at θ = 0 sat on a grid column, which is why it worked. Fixed in r1q: cone and torus traces seed inside the angular window the pair's box subtends about the axis. The window is exact whenever all eight box corners lie within an arc under π; otherwise the full circle is kept, shifted by π on a cone's far nappe. Reproducer `tests/briefs/repro/union_splitter_off_seam`, 5 s, OCCT fuse 447471.2819.
18. **A certified volume was 48 mm³ wrong.** The G6 stator rings united with one NGV were certified at [459629.2165, 459629.2217], but OCCT reads the exported union at 459581.0774, and its own fuse agrees. `tests/eval/occ_oracle.py faces` compares each face's divergence contribution with a fine OCCT triangulation, and two planar NGV end caps disagreed: area 21.95 against 12.27, and 8.58 against 0.133. `pl_bz` and `bz_j` integrated a Bezier boundary edge over its whole curve and ignored the edge's domain. The ring cut had split the caps' airfoil edges, so each trimmed edge counted in full, and the Richardson-style slack never covers a missing piece of boundary. Fixed in r1q: the 3-point Gauss rule runs over [lo, hi], which is bit-identical for full edges. Reproducer `tests/briefs/repro/vol_trimmed_bezier_cap` now certifies [459581.0748, 459581.0800].
19. **Validation kept unresolved boxes that lie outside a face.** The full G6 impeller (hub, 12 blades, 12 splitters) was rejected with `face-face-uncertain` on a blade's flat trailing face against the last hub cone. `tests/k3/zffp.bend` replays the validator's face-face check. The generic tracer succeeded with one branch but left two unresolved boxes at z ≈ 65.751–65.755, while the trailing face ends at z = 65.745. A box was accepted only if it lay on a shared edge, never because it lay outside one of the two faces. Fixed in r1r: a box disjoint from either face's box, or certified outside either face, is dropped. The full impeller now evaluates in 394 s, against more than 4 h before: one solid, genus 1, 668 faces, [523825.84, 523830.46], OCCT fuse 523828.1498.
20. **Cylinder membership used one coarse band for a whole face.** The 29-NGV stator was rejected with `edge-face-uncertain`: an NGV cap edge ends 0.0846 mm across the y = 0 seam from the outer ring's half-cylinder, inside that face's 0.111 mm uncertainty band. `tests/k3/zefp.bend` replays the edge-face check and shows the face: 2,031 edges and 64,868 uv segments. `tests/k3/zdv.bend` shows that no traced chain deviates more than 1e-3 mm. The band came from sampling each curved edge at a fixed 32 uv segments (0.027 mm sag on long edges wrapping the NGV leading edge), times 4, applied around every segment. In r1s, exact edges (seams and coaxial circles, on the surface to 1e-9) get a band of only their own sampling deviation. In r1t, each curved edge is sampled at 32 to 2048 segments until its deviation is below max(face slack, 1e-6(1+r)), and the segments are binned by angle (512 bins), so a membership query reads only the bins its ray and its band touch instead of all 64,868 segments.
21. **An edge sample one ulp past its domain evaluated to the origin.** With 20 fixed, the 29-NGV stator committed, but 1,500 mm³ too high. United with the rings one NGV at a time, 23 angles gave exactly 459581.08 and six gave 200–300 mm³ more. On one bad NGV patch face, `tests/k3/zvol.bend` showed a trimmed root edge whose 48- and 96-sample patch integrals were wrong while 32 and 64 were exact. The last sample, t0 + 48·dt, came out at 1.0000000000000002; `bez3_eval` rejects it, and `EF.ev` turned the failure into the point (0, 0, 0). Fixed in r1u: `EF.ev` clamps overshoots of up to 1e-12 into a Bezier's [0, 1], and the patch integrator clamps its samples to the edge domain. All 29 angles now agree, and the full stator certifies [485051.919, 485052.071] in 841 s. Reproducer `tests/briefs/repro/union_stator_29_ngv`.

## Found and fixed 2026-11-22 (running clearances)

22. **The compressor tip clearance could not get a positive lower bound.** `clearance 1903 2301` (the G5 blade against the front cover) printed `[0, 0.2727]`. That failed the running-clearance check at −0.1024, even though the witness gap was larger than the 0.1024 mm closure. The probe `tests/k3/zcl.bend` now prints each term of a pair's bound. After 99,841 pops, 12,800 blade-cone pairs were left, all at 0. Every remaining blade box lay entirely outside the blade: a Bezier-patch face was refined by cutting its 3D bounding box into cubes, and nothing tested whether the surface passed through a cube. The shroud cone (slope 0.04, apex 577 mm away) was bounded by its infinite surface, which crosses those empty cubes. Fixed in r1w. A Bezier-patch face with no implicit is refined in (u, v): each piece's box is the hull of its sub-patch (`BP.bq_box`), intersected with the face box. A piece is critical only if the separation direction can be normal to both `bq_du` and `bq_dv`; boundary minima belong to the edge and vertex entities. Cone and cylinder faces are also bounded by their true axial extent, taken from their boundary edges (plus the apex when it lies in the face box), not by the infinite surface. The same query now certifies [0.224, 0.2727] in 29 s, and the running clearance is +0.1216. Reproducer `tests/briefs/repro/clearance_blade_cone_shroud`. The first cut of the axial-extent bound used each edge curve's interval box over [0, 1]. A full circle is stored as one rational Bezier whose weight crosses zero, so that box is NaN. A NaN bound compares false against the incumbent, and the pair was dropped. That gave the parallel cylinders of `clearance_general_position` the bound [6.4999, 6.5] instead of 0.5, and the threaded part's wall 5.5 instead of 1.25. The extent now comes from the face's certified box, tightened by the edges only when their boxes are finite, and a non-finite bound counts as 0.

23. **The diffuser mesh folded where a vane meets its end plane.** The G5 diffuser had 36 edges used twice in the same direction, on 6 of its 30 straightener vanes. Each vane's ruled face (bicubic) and its planar end section at r = 52.9 both contained the same near-zero-area triangle, built from three consecutive samples of their shared edge. That edge is an iso-line of the patch. Inverting its samples to (u, v) gives u = 1 ± 1e-13, so the uv ear clipper saw a random tiny orientation and sometimes clipped an ear that the end plane clipped too. Whether it did depended on the vane's angle: the reproducer union with the vane at 6° or 18° is clean, at 54° it has 2 bad edges, and at 66° it has 12. The closed-mesh test compared only the multiset of forward edges with the multiset of reversed edges, so the fold passed and the part was written. Fixed in r1x. The tessellator snaps inverted Bezier-patch parameters within 1e-9 of 0 or 1 onto the boundary, and `closed` also rejects any directed edge used twice, so a fold is refused (`tess-mesh-not-closed`) rather than written. Reproducer `tests/briefs/repro/tess_vane_fold`.

24. **A trimmed patch was bounded as its whole patch.** The G6 turbine blade is a skin intersected with a revolve at the tip radius. Against the shroud cylinder at TT + 0.15 it certified [0, 0.15]. The (u, v) refinement of defect 22 used the untrimmed patch, whose section runs out to TT + 2 and through the shroud. Fixed in r1y. A patch piece that no boundary edge of the face reaches, and whose centre point classifies outside the trimmed face (`Mm.member`), is dropped. If no trim curve crosses the piece, the whole piece lies on one side of the trim. The query now certifies [0.1499979, 0.1499997] in 22 s, and the defect-22 cases are unchanged. Reproducer `tests/briefs/repro/clearance_trimmed_blade`.

## Found and fixed 2026-11-23 (STL at manufacturing tolerance, modal mesh levels)

25. **The G4 impeller STL at preset 3 faulted the kernel.** `stl 1 impeller.stl 3` ended with `bend: memory fault (machine stack overflow?)` after 278 s at 11.4 GB RSS. It failed the same way with an unlimited C stack and with the display profile. The trigger is the 0.01 rad angle tolerance alone:
    - chord 0.1 with angle 0.01 faults;
    - chord 0.05 with angle 0.02 writes 1,648,344 triangles at 3.5 GB;
    - chord 0.1 with angle 0.02 writes 1,497,116 triangles at 3.1 GB.

    That is about 2 KB per triangle. Most of it is the OBJ text: `body_q` renders every vertex and face as a Bend `String` (one heap cell per character), and `stl` parses that string back into the same lists before writing facets. Fixed in r1z. `TS.pri_mesh` returns the welded triangles and placed points directly, with the same closure, budget and refusal semantics. The CLI writes STL from them for every non-revolved body. On a 5,736-triangle part the r1y and r1z files are byte-identical. The impeller at preset 3 now writes 5,639,248 triangles in 2,465 s at 5.2 GB. The file is closed, with 0 unpaired edges, and its mesh volume is 2,530,939.39 against the STEP volume of 2,530,977.89 (−0.0015%). G4 run 2 grades 50/50. `tools/grade/g4.py` now checks STL with numpy: the same rounding and edge pairing, identical counts on all 14 G4 parts and on a mesh with one facet removed (3 unpaired edges in both), and about 4× faster.
26. **A modal mesh refusal arrived after hours of solving.** G5 turbine blade modes at H 0.65 ran for 28,341 s and then printed `refused mesh-uncertified`. `modes_s` solved all three mesh levels before looking at any level's certification flag. In r1z, all three meshes are built first, and the eigen solves run only when every mesh certifies. A refusal now names the level that failed (`the level h=... mesh`).

Open-defect reproducers live in `tests/briefs/defects/`, run with `REPRO_DIR=tests/briefs/defects python3 tests/briefs/repro/run.py`. Each moves into `tests/briefs/repro` when its fix lands.
